# Mandate

**An operating system for public affairs, for European innovative companies.**

Public affairs is treated by startups as a cost centre. It is an investment, and
almost none of them have the capacity to do it. Mandate is a bench of domain
experts — *brains* — that watch the public sphere on a company's behalf and tell
it what moved and why that matters to **it**, specifically.

## The bench

| | brain | the question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| ① | **capital** | What can the state put into this company, on what terms, through which instrument, by when, and who administers it? |
| ② | **policy** | What rules land on this company, when do they bite, and what has to change? |

Two are hired. Ten more are planned — what is moving and which way, where you can
be heard, who holds the pen, what the institutions say they want, the state as a
customer, what stands between you and the market, what could hit you, what worked
for companies like you, which rooms to be in, and what to co-sign.

They are not ten systems. They are ten questions over one spine: **one company
profile · one register of who decides · one corpus · one radar.**

## Layout

```
capital/    the funding brain   (was AsgerMG/Wincent — full history preserved)
policy/     the policy brain    (was AsgerMG/Regina  — full history preserved)
```

Each arrives whole, with its own history, so `git log` still answers "why is this
like this" — which is the most valuable thing either repo contains. Restructuring
happens inside, afterwards, with ordinary moves.

## Principles

- **A brain owes an opinion, not a digest.** Guidance, not a summary of the law.
- **Watch and tell.** They notice and explain. They do not draft or file.
- **Nothing ships that cannot cite a source and a date.** In public affairs a
  confident wrong answer costs more than silence.
- **Data lives in Railway.** Generated artefacts are produced at build time, not
  committed — the two repos carried 900 MB of regenerated JSON between them.
