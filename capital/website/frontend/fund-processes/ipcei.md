# IPCEI (Important Projects of Common European Interest)

**Channel code:** `ipcei`
**Run by:** Member states jointly, approved by the European Commission (DG COMP)
under Article 107(3)(b) TFEU. Money is paid by **national** governments; the
Commission authorises the state aid.
**Reference:** https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/state-aid/ipcei_en

## What it funds
Large, cross-border value chains the EU deems strategically essential:
microelectronics, batteries, hydrogen, cloud/edge (next-gen cloud
infrastructure and services), health, and more. The **conviction tier's top
floor** — funding can run to hundreds of millions per participant and finances
R&D&I through first industrial deployment.

## Who is eligible
- Companies participating in an approved IPCEI value chain, nominated through
  their **member state's** process (national expression-of-interest calls).
- The project must show market failure, significant spillovers across the EU,
  and co-financing by the company. It is selected for **strategic relevance**,
  not grant pedigree — most participants did not climb a grant ladder to get in.
- Public money lands on the specific **legal entity** in the consortium; group
  structure matters (subsidiaries often receive, not listed parents).

## Process and timeline
1. National authority runs an expression-of-interest / matching process for a
   given IPCEI wave.
2. Selected companies join the integrated project; member states notify the
   Commission.
3. **Commission state-aid approval** (DG COMP) — the gating step.
4. National grant agreements; multi-year disbursement against milestones.

**Realistic elapsed time:** **years.** This runs on the policy calendar and
involves your member state directly. Begin while you are Series A–B if this is
your path.

## Funding rate and ticket
Set per project and capped by a "funding gap" methodology; aid covers eligible
costs up to the gap. Tickets are very large; clawback applies if the project
over-performs (a success-claw mechanism).

## What evaluators weight
Strategic fit to a declared European value chain; ambition beyond state of the
art; cross-border spillovers; the company's own co-investment and capability.

## How a founder should act on this
- If you sit in a strategic value chain (chips, batteries, hydrogen, cloud,
  health), say so in every interaction and get on your member state's radar now.
- A competitor in an IPCEI is positioned for a decade of strategic-autonomy
  funding and is hard to dislodge. Map which value chain they entered.
- Keep the receiving entity's records clean — investors and the state both look
  the entity up.

## At a glance
*Structured trade-off snapshot. Drives the instrument-comparison table; keep each line to one clause.*
- Ticket: Very large, member-state routed: often €10M–€1B+ per project
- Speed: Years: national pre-notification plus EC approval under Art. 107(3)(b)
- You give up: Requires a national government to sponsor and co-fund you; a long political process; first-of-a-kind and spillover obligations that reduce flexibility
- Best for: Strategic value-chain projects (batteries, hydrogen, microelectronics, cloud) with a member state behind you
