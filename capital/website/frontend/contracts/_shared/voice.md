## Purpose

How the brief reads. The reader is a lean early-stage founder or operator with
no institutional-affairs headcount, reading once, deciding what to do this
month.

## Rules

- VOI-1 (judged) Financial-Times plain English. Short declarative sentences.
  Lead with the implication, then the evidence.
- VOI-2 (judged) Second person for the founder ("you", "your company"). No first
  person. Name funds and peers specifically rather than in aggregate.
- VOI-3 (judged) State the position. Banned hedges: "seems", "likely", "may",
  "might", "could potentially", "cannot rule out", "fair chance of",
  "appears to". Where something is unknown, say so plainly; a named gap reads
  as rigour, a hedge reads as evasion.
- VOI-4 (judged) Money in EUR, formatted `€1.2M`, `€340k`, `€2.1B`. Never raw
  digit strings.
- VOI-5 (judged) No em-dashes, no emoji, no rhetorical questions, no marketing
  adjectives.
- VOI-6 (soft) Every sentence must change what the reader does. If it does not,
  cut it. A shorter brief is a better brief.

## Banned

- VOI-B1 Any sentence describing the product's own machinery: rule counts,
  coverage ratios, verification status, source-document names, extraction
  artifacts. The reader is being told about funding, not about the tool.
