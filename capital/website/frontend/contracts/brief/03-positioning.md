## Purpose

How this company should frame itself to fit the political moment its funders
are operating in. The section that turns a list of doors into an argument the
founder can actually make when they walk through one.

## Rules

- POS-1 (hard) One paragraph, at most 200 words.
- POS-2 (judged) Name the real people or institutions setting the agenda, from
  the supplied evidence only. Where the evidence is too thin to be certain a
  person holds a relevant role, refer to the role or institution instead.
  Never guess a name.
- POS-3 (judged) Every named person carries their role and their relevance to
  this company in the same sentence. A name attached to a stance the reader
  cannot attribute is noise.

  **evidence:** a brief named an individual and a stance in an appositive
  clause with no role given, leaving the reader unable to tell whose position
  was being described or why it mattered. The 130-word cap in force at the
  time made attribution the first thing compressed out.

- POS-6 (judged) Where the evidence carries dated movement on a fund in the
  founder's own table, prefer it to the topic-level agenda and carry the date.
  A founder acts on "the fund in your row 2 opens a EUR 380M call in Q4", not
  on "the Commission is consolidating programmes". Where the fund-movement
  block is empty, their funds are national schemes the policy corpus does not
  track: use the agenda and do NOT imply movement on their specific funds.

  **evidence:** until 2026-08-02 the corpus could only be queried by topic and
  sector, so this section could only ever describe the landscape. Signals are
  now attached to specific instruments, and 12 of 12 test archetypes have at
  least one fund on the table carrying dated movement.

- POS-4 (judged) The framing advice must fit how this company actually reaches
  its market. Use the founder's stated commercial model: who pays, who buys,
  through what channel. Where that is unknown, write about the technology and
  the policy priority, never about a customer.
- POS-5 (judged) Say what specifically in the stated priorities this company's
  work maps onto. A paragraph that could be sent to any company in the sector
  has failed.
- POS-6 (soft) Advisory register. Never sycophantic, never inventing a meeting,
  relationship or introduction that does not exist.

## Banned

- POS-B1 Naming a customer, buyer, sales channel or go-to-market the founder
  has not stated (GND-7).
- POS-B2 Recommending a framing register for a fund that is not published.

## Worked example

### Pass

> Frame the work against the national programme's stated priority for adoption
> at scale rather than the EU deep-tech agenda: the agency's published plan
> weights deployment evidence, and your paying users are the evidence it asks
> for.

### Fail — breaks POS-4 and POS-B1

> Pitch on institutional deployment and outcomes for the buyers who procure at
> scale.

Written for a procurement-led business. Applied to a company that sells
directly to individuals, every noun in it is wrong, and nothing in the
founder's answers supported any of them.
