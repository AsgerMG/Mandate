## Purpose

The four or five sentences a founder would keep if they read nothing else:
where they stand, which door to try first, and what the money is actually
worth to them. This is the section that decides whether the rest gets read.

## Rules

- BL-1 (hard) Four to five bullets. Not three, not six.
- BL-2 (hard) At most 40 words per bullet.
- BL-3 (judged) Simple prose. One idea per bullet, said in ordinary sentences.
  No stacked clauses, no semicolons, no parenthetical asides. If a bullet needs
  two sentences to be clear, use two sentences. Plain text with no bullet
  marker: the interface renders the list.

  **evidence:** a 25-word cap was in force and fired on four of five bullets in
  a measured run. The bullets were not too ambitious, the limit was too tight
  for what a bullet has to carry, and the writer met it by compressing clauses
  together rather than by saying less.
- BL-4 (judged) The first bullet opens from the founder's own traced footprint
  set against the comparable evidence. Where nothing is traced, that absence
  is the opening fact, stated plainly, not a footnote.
- BL-5 (judged) The middle bullets name which of the published funds is the
  right first move and why it is that one rather than another.
- BL-6 (judged) The final bullet states the commercial payoff in concrete terms:
  a grant amount, a guarantee that lowers cost of capital, an equity stake
  from a public investor, a tax credit. Never "access to funding".
- BL-7 (soft) Each bullet earns its place by changing a decision. Restating
  the table in prose does not.

## Banned

- BL-B1 Naming a fund that is not in the published table.
- BL-B2 Comparative urgency about other companies (see GND-8).

## Worked example

### Pass

> You have no traced public funding, while two comparable companies in your
> sub-sector drew a combined €4.1M from the same national scheme since 2022.

### Fail — breaks BL-6

> A first award could bring in meaningful non-dilutive funding and improve
> access to further capital.

No figure, no instrument, no mechanism. "Meaningful" and "access to further
capital" describe nothing the reader can act on.
