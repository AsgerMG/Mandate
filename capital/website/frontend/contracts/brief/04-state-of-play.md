## Purpose

To tell the founder that the doors above are not fixed. The funds in their
table sit inside political cycles and industrial agendas, and when those move,
the gates and the winning positioning move with them. The section exists to
make the reader understand that this is a moving target worth tracking, and
that tracking it is what we do.

A previous version reported recent policy developments and then conceded that
none of them changed anything today. That is a newsletter, not a decision
input, and it is the failure this contract is written against.

## Shape

Two movements, in order, in a single paragraph.

**First — the moving ground, made specific to this company.** The funds
published above are shaped by political cycles and industrial agendas. Say
what is actually moving in the supplied evidence, and say what it would change
for THESE funds: a gate that could tighten or loosen, a priority that could
shift what a winning application argues, a programme whose shape is being
decided. Concrete and grounded, never a general survey of European policy.

**Second — the standing offer.** Wincent reads those signals continuously,
anticipates the changes and opens the right doors. Partner with us to play the
political momentum to your advantage.

The second movement is fixed copy, appended in code, and is never written by
the model. It renders as its own block.

## Rules

- SOP-0 (judged) Lead with movement on the founder's OWN funds where the
  evidence has it, dated, before any landscape-level development. The section
  exists to say the doors are not fixed; a door they are actually standing at
  moving is worth more than a door elsewhere moving. Where no fund-level
  movement is supplied, say the landscape is moving without implying their
  specific funds are.

- SOP-1 (hard) One paragraph, at most 200 words.
- SOP-2 (hard) The closing offer is appended separately and must never appear
  inside the analytical paragraph.

  **evidence:** the two were string-concatenated into one field, so a section
  of analysis ended mid-paragraph in a sales line.

- SOP-3 (judged) Every development mentioned is tied to what it would mean for a
  fund in the published table. A development with no stated consequence for
  this company does not belong.
- SOP-4 (judged) Grounded only in the supplied recency-sorted evidence. Where
  there is nothing recent for this sector, say so plainly in one sentence
  rather than reaching for general policy news.
- SOP-5 (judged) Never state or imply that a change alters eligibility today
  unless the evidence says so. The claim is about direction of travel.
- SOP-6 (soft) Written so the reader finishes it understanding that their
  funding landscape has weather, and that someone is watching it.

## Banned

- SOP-B1 A closing summary or sign-off of the model's own.
- SOP-B2 Reporting a development and then conceding it changes nothing. If it
  changes nothing, it is not the development to report.

## Worked example

### Pass

> The programme behind two of the funds above is being consolidated into a
> larger instrument, with its scope decided over the next budget round: the
> thematic priorities that currently favour your work are exactly what is
> being renegotiated, so an application built on today's framing may need a
> different argument by the time the next call opens.

### Fail — breaks SOP-3 and SOP-B2

> Two think tanks published assessments questioning whether the proposed fund
> can deliver. Neither development changes eligibility today, but both mark
> where EU-level grant design is heading.

Nothing here touches a fund in this founder's table, and the sentence concedes
its own irrelevance.
