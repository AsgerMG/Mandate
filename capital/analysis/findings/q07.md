The EIC+EDF pair is the single strongest co-funding bridge in the corpus: 15 companies appear in both instrument families, confirming that the EU's deep-tech grant mechanism and its defense-oriented program are the most common pipeline pairing. EIC+EIB (10 companies) and EIC+Innovation Fund (5) are the next strongest pairs. No company spans more than 3 of the 7 source families. Notably absent: there are very few EIB+EDF pairs (0 in the data), suggesting that the EU's venture-debt facility and its defense program operate in separate ecosystems with minimal overlap. The IPCEI family shows almost no co-funding with EIC (0 pairs), EDF (0), or EIB (0) — consistent with IPCEI being a member-state-led programme with different company eligibility than the European Commission-run instruments. The co-funding "pipeline" hypothesis (EIC → EIB venture debt) is visible but weak: only 10 companies flow EIC → EIB, out of 2,188 EIC-funded companies, a pipeline rate of just 0.5%. This suggests EIB venture debt is not systematically deployed as a follow-on to EIC grants.
