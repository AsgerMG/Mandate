<!-- What this shows -->
EU public funding is highly concentrated within each instrument. The EIT has the highest Gini coefficient (0.88), meaning a tiny fraction of EIT-backed companies receive almost all the capital — unsurprising given EIT's large-consortium model where a few KIC hubs account for nearly all reported amounts. IPCEI (0.69) and EDF (0.67) follow, consistent with these programmes funding a small number of large industrial projects per call. The EIC, despite being the most company-diverse instrument (2,188 companies), still has a Gini of 0.49 — its top decile captures 32% of total EIC grant value. Innovation Fund sits at 0.60, driven by a handful of very large low-carbon infrastructure grants. EIB Gini cannot be computed because the dataset contains no non-zero debt amounts — debt_amount_eur fields are uniformly NULL or zero for this family, which is a known data-collection gap. The broad takeaway: no EU instrument distributes capital evenly; all exhibit long tails dominated by large legacy institutions or industrial primes.
