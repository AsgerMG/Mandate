<!-- What this shows -->
Every measurable instrument family concentrates capital severely. The EIT KIC programme has a Gini coefficient of 0.98 and a top-decile share of 100 percent: essentially all reported EIT capital flows to a handful of KIC hub entities. Horizon Europe Pillar II, despite being the broadest programme by company count at 16,856 entities, still posts a Gini of 0.80 with the top decile capturing 72 percent of grant value. The national development banks roll up to a Gini of 0.76 and a top-decile share of 63 percent, similar in shape to the EU-level instruments rather than markedly more distributed. IPCEI (0.69, top decile 51 percent) and EDF (0.67, top decile 56 percent) follow, consistent with these programmes funding a small number of large industrial projects per call. The Innovation Fund sits at 0.60, driven by a handful of very large low-carbon infrastructure grants. The Chips JU posts 0.57 across 36 companies, with the top decile capturing 41 percent.

The most distributed instruments are the EIC and the Horizon EU Partnerships, both at a Gini of 0.49 with top-decile shares of 32 to 37 percent. Even there, the broad EIC concentrates one euro in three within the largest decile of recipients. EIB cannot be evaluated on the same axis because debt amounts are systematically absent from the extracted data.

<!-- Scope qualifiers -->
- IPCEI grant amounts are partially redacted in the source data. The computed Gini understates true concentration.
- EIB Gini is not available. No usable debt-amount values were extracted. This is a known data-collection gap.
- EIT grant amounts may reflect consortium-level totals allocated to the coordinator, which inflates the top-decile share.
