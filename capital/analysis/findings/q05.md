623 companies hold the STEP Seal, equivalent to 2.9 percent of the 21,689-company corpus. The seal is distributed sharply unevenly across instruments. The Horizon Europe Partnerships have the highest STEP rate at 79 percent, the EIC follows at 76 percent, and Horizon Europe Pillar II at 67 percent. EDF posts 64 percent, Chips JU 56 percent, and the Innovation Fund 40 percent. The bottom of the table is the more telling read: the national development banks STEP-tag only 2 percent of their cohort, EIT 1 percent, and IPCEI 4 percent. EIB sits at 10 percent.

The pattern is largely explained by the Seal's design: it was introduced specifically to flag EIC portfolio companies as strategic, so the EIC overlap is structural rather than informative. The high EDF rate (64 percent) is more interesting because it indicates that defence-related companies frequently appear across the EU's strategic technology list even though EDF predates the STEP framework. By sector, defence (11 percent) and space (10 percent) post the highest rates, with digital and the national development bank cohort the lowest. The low rate among national development bank recipients is the cleanest signal that the Seal targets EU-level strategic instruments rather than national programmes.

- STEP Seal data was loaded from a user-supplied dataset dated 2026-05-22. Matching was performed by PIC code and by fuzzy name. Match confidence varies. A small number of companies in the corpus had no PIC and were not matchable.
- The STEP Seal is an EU Commission label, not an independent quality assessment. The high EIC overlap is structural.
- Country-level rates among very small EU members are noisy because of small sample sizes.
