Of 21,689 companies in the corpus, 1,564 (7.2 percent) appear across two or more EU funding instrument families. Restricting to entity type company strips out research organisations and public bodies and leaves 734 cross-instrument companies, the cleaner signal for startups and scaleups. The all-entities count is heavily inflated by Horizon Europe Pillar II consortium leadership, where research organisations frequently appear alongside companies in cluster-funded projects.

By raw co-occurrence the dominant pair is EIC together with Horizon Europe Pillar II (444 companies), reflecting that most EIC-funded companies also feature somewhere in the Pillar II cluster ingestion. The second-strongest pair is Horizon Europe Pillar II together with the national development banks (158 companies), which is the strongest evidence in the corpus of national programmes funding the same firms that EU instruments do. The historically discussed EIC-to-EDF defence bridge is present but smaller, at 44 companies. IPCEI continues to show limited overlap with EIC or EDF, consistent with its status as a state-aid-notified member-state-led instrument.

When ranked by total declared capital, the top cross-instrument companies are dominated by research institutes (SINTEF AS in Norway, VTT in Finland) and large industrial primes, not by emerging ventures.

<!-- Scope qualifiers -->
- Coordinator-only loading means champion counts are minimum estimates. A company that coordinated one EDF project but participated in ten others appears in the corpus only once.
- Capital totals mix grant and debt amounts across instruments with different reporting bases.
- The all-entities count overstates the startup or scaleup picture. The companies-only view is the cleaner read.
