Of 3,101 companies in the corpus, 81 (2.6%) appear across two or more EU funding instrument families — a cohort we call the cross-instrument champions. By source-family count the leaders are CEA (3 families: EIC, Chips JU, EDF), Lynred (3: EIC, EDF, EIC), and SINTEF AS (3: EIC, Chips JU, EDF), all large multi-domain research organisations or defense-primes. When re-ranked by total declared capital, THALES (€449M across EIC+EDF), Fincantieri (€401M), and Airbus (€358M) dominate — reflecting that France's defense primes are the financial heavyweights even if not the most source-diverse. Note: an earlier version of the methodology doc cited "THALES 9 sources, CEA 8, Airbus 7" — those figures counted raw source-call values (e.g., edf_ra_2021 + edf_da_2021 each counted separately); after source-family normalisation the correct counts are THALES 2 families, CEA 3, Airbus 2. The EIC+EDF pair is by far the most common co-occurrence (15 companies), confirming that the defense-tech bridge is the strongest documented pipeline in this dataset. Sector-wise, defense (22% of champions vs 6% of corpus) is dramatically over-represented in this cohort, and life sciences is under-represented, consistent with defense programmes (EDF, IPCEI) structurally favouring large primes who also receive EIC grants.
