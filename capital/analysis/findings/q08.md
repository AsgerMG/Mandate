On a per-capita basis the small economies dominate. Iceland tops the table at 132.5 companies per million residents, followed by Cyprus (124.6), Luxembourg (120.0), Slovenia (109.5), and Estonia (101.4). The bottom of the developed-economy sample is led by France (24.6 per million), Bulgaria (28.4), Italy (29.8), Germany (31.2), and Croatia (36.7). The pattern is consistent: small ecosystems with high EU programme uptake outperform large national champions on the per-capita ratio, even when the absolute company counts in those large economies are an order of magnitude higher.

Comparing the corpus share against approximate startup-ecosystem size from Sifted 2023, Spain is heavily over-represented (15.7 percent of the corpus versus 6.0 percent of the ecosystem share), driven by deep CDTI coverage in the national development bank layer. The United Kingdom is the most under-represented large economy (3.6 percent of the corpus versus 20.9 percent of the ecosystem share), a structural consequence of post-Brexit exclusion from the main EIC pool. Germany is moderately under-represented (12.3 percent versus 15.4 percent) because much of German participation in IPCEI hydrogen and battery projects is as non-coordinator participant rather than as project lead.

The corpus is structurally incapable of detecting consumer-software, SaaS, or pure-platform companies. None of the EU instruments tracked here target consumer software.
