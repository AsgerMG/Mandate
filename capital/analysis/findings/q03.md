<!-- What this shows -->
Country and sector concentration patterns vary sharply by instrument. EIC company counts are dominated by life sciences (1,025), cleantech (513), and deep tech and hardware (395). The Innovation Fund is essentially monocultural at 236 cleantech companies, reflecting its mandate as the EU Emissions Trading System revenue vehicle. EDF is 174 defence companies plus 11 space and a handful of life-sciences entities, the latter mostly multi-domain research organisations like DLR and ONERA whose first-appearance sector overrides the EDF programme context. IPCEI concentrates in cleantech (174), deep tech (76), and life sciences (31), tracking the IPCEI hydrogen, battery, and microelectronics decisions. Chips JU is smaller than expected at 36 coordinator companies, with deep tech and hardware leading. The national development banks load most heavily in deep tech and hardware (2,033 companies), reflecting that the German HTGF, French Bpifrance, and Spanish CDTI all anchor in hardware-intensive engineering rather than pure software.

Spain is the largest country contributor at 3,379 companies, ahead of Germany (2,633), Italy (1,759), and France (1,681). The Spanish position is driven by the national development bank CDTI, which contributes 1,741 of the Spanish entries. Nordic countries cluster in cleantech and life sciences. Eastern European countries are largely absent from the high-value coordinator-only instruments.

<!-- Scope qualifiers -->
- Sector classification is first-appearance-wins from the EIC pipeline. Multi-domain research organisations carry whichever label the EIC classifier assigned on first load.
- Country is the company headquarters jurisdiction, not the project execution country. Multi-site organisations appear under their HQ.
- The Spanish lead is amplified by CDTI coverage. Without the national development bank layer, Germany would lead.
