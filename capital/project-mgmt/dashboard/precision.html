Where it goes wrong

Four stages. We are excellent at the first, and give the accuracy back at the next two — both of them cuts we chose, and neither of them measured until this week.

1 · We find the right fund —%

Out of roughly 1,100 funds, our shortlist of about 100 contains the right one almost every time. This part genuinely works.

2 · Some are ruled out before we look —%

Before anything ranks, we cap how many kinds of funding and how many funds per kind we will consider at all. That drops roughly a fifth of the right answers, and no amount of ranking can reach them afterwards.

3 · We pass on only part of what is left —%

Only the top — of that shortlist reaches whatever picks the final five. The right fund survives the cut fewer than half the time — so more often than not, we are choosing from a list the answer is not on.

4 · Five are chosen and shown —%

Chosen today by —. Whatever does the choosing cannot name a fund it was never shown, which is why this number can never be better than the one above it.

The finding

Two cuts, not one, and together they cost us more than half our accuracy. We hold the right fund —% of the time and put —% of it in front of the thing that chooses. No ranker can beat that ceiling — a flawless one, given those rows, still tops out there.

Worth being clear about

The AI ranker is built, measured, and switched off. Every number above is our own scoring, not an AI choosing. We have not turned it on because it has not yet beaten the simple version — and the reason it has not is the 44% above: it is being handed a list the answer is usually not on. That is what the work below is fixing.

What we found this week

Guesswork replaced by measurement. Three of these ruled something out, which is what stopped us spending on it.

fixed We were destroying our own fund descriptions. A text bug cut one 725-character description down to 36. Of 133 funds, 51 were passed on with no readable line saying what they pay for. Now 1.
found We label 95% of funds with a one-line summary that is wrong half the time. Every fund carries a line like “mostly software & AI”. It names a real majority in only 46% of cases, and it rules out the correct fund 48% of the time. Stop writing it and the number of defensible options in front of the chooser goes from 7 of 18 to 15 of 18. Costs 25 cents to confirm.
ruled out Reordering the shortlist does not help. Every sensible reordering was measured. The best gained 1 point on the split that counts and lost accuracy deeper down.
ruled out A longer list does not help. Doubling the list changed nothing and scored slightly worse. The question is which funds we show, not how many.
ruled out A more expensive AI does not help. Tripling its thinking budget made the result worse, not better. It is not short of intelligence. It is short of options.
ruled out Better follow-up questions did not help either. We asked a logistics-software company a sharper question. It answered correctly. The wrong fund still came first.
protected Two months of paid work sat in one place. 456,000 rows that could not have been rebuilt, with no backup covering them. Now backed up, verified, and off the machine.
