Are we naming the right fund?

We put a handful of funds in front of a founder. This page tracks one thing: how often the fund they would actually have won is one of them.

Our own test · — companies whose funding we know —%

How often the fund a company actually won is among the — we put in front of them. The near-term goal is 60%. Measured end to end on our own test set, not read off production — it is the number we are building, and it moves only when the product does.

Where the right fund is lost

Measured end to end on 897 companies whose funding we already know, following the right answer the whole way through. One test, one group, every stage.

One loss is now bigger than everything else combined. After the AI has ranked the funds, the list is cut to eight before the write-up — and that costs thirty points. It was eleven a day ago; widening the list the AI sees pushed the loss down onto this cut rather than removing it.

Widening the AI's list is done, and cost nothing. It sees forty funds instead of eighteen, which lifted the share of founders whose fund it can even consider from 50% to 69%. Measured across 897 companies, not one lost anything by it. But the gain is a ceiling: it only becomes accuracy if the AI promotes a good fund up the list, and our free test runs without the AI, so it cannot yet show that.

Why 60% and not 90%. Getting the right fund in front of a founder is two things multiplied: it has to survive our own filters (83% today), and the AI has to pick it out of forty (~51% today). For 90%, the AI would have to be right about 95% of the time — at today's filters the arithmetic does not reach 90% at all. 60% is reachable and every step to it is identified.

And it is not a data problem. Not one of the founders we lose had a fund that was closed. Roughly nineteen of the twenty-three points lost before the AI is even asked are our own encoding errors.
