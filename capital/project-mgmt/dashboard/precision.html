The architecture, in six steps

A founder types, we build a candidate subset, a model interrogates that subset and then ranks it. Two of the six steps are where the accuracy is lost, and both are marked.

  1. 1

    The founder answers fixed questions

    works

    One screen, about a dozen fields. What they do, where they are, what stage, what the money is for.

  2. 2

    Our code builds the subset

    works

    Gates and channels narrow ~1,088 funds to a candidate pool — median 105, range 25 to 185. The fund that actually paid out is in that pool 97% of the time. This part is genuinely good and is not the problem.

  3. 3

    The model asks what it still needs

    does not exist

    Today a separate planner writes the second screen before anything has looked at the candidates, so it is guessing which ambiguity matters. It should be the ranker asking, because the ranker is holding the shortlist and can see which two rows it cannot separate.

    A question is allowed only if all three hold: it resolves a requirement a candidate fund actually imposes, in that fund's own published terms; the value is genuinely unknown; and knowing it would change which funds get named. The third leg is the one that cuts. If nothing qualifies, the right output is no questions — padding to a fixed number is the same defect as filling six fund seats.

  4. 4

    The founder answers those

    wrong questions

    The screen exists and works. What it asks is written by the wrong component.

  5. 5

    The model ranks, holding everything

    starved

    It is handed 18 rows, not the subset. The pool is cut before the model ever runs, and at 18 rows the right fund is present 44.2% of the time. And of those 18, a mean of 2.1 survive the disqualifiers our own prompt states — while we demand it name six. Retrieval, its route into the embedded database, has fired 0 times in 102 calls.

  6. 6

    The five or six go into the memo

    works

    The writer is not the problem, and is deliberately untouched.

Two numbers, and they are the whole story

The subset holds the answer 97% of the time. The list we hand the model holds it 44%. We throw away more than half our own accuracy in a cut nobody measured — and then require the model to name six funds from a list where two are defensible.

This is why paying more at step 5 cannot work: a perfect ranker on an 18-row list scores 44.2%. Measured accordingly — tripling the thinking budget, €0.016 to €0.053 a brief, moved the score down.

Read this before comparing 30.3% with 27.6%

The drop is not a regression. On 9 August the answer key was replaced. The old one could only name 390 of the funds the product offers, in 9 countries; the new one names 611, in 28 — it had never heard of CDTI, Neotec or Innovate UK. Measured head-to-head on the same corpus and the same three seeds, precision rose on every seed while the exam got harder. The two eras are plotted separately and are never joined by a line, because the numbers either side are answers to different questions.

Two architectures, and which one is being measured

Every number on this page above 11 August was produced by the DETERMINISTIC ranker — a score, sorted, cut to six. That approach is finished: ten routes measured, ten zeros, and an oracle holding the answer key gained one case in six hundred.

The architecture is now an LLM ranker. Our code builds the subset — 18 to 185 funds, the right one in it 97% of the time — and a model reads the founder, the funds and their own words, and chooses. Its first audited run: twelve companies, €0.21, all chose, none fell back.

What the four levers turned out to be

One day, four investigations, €10.48. Three of the four candidate explanations for the ranker's errors are now dead, which is the useful half.

LeverVerdictWhat was measured
A longer list dead 33 rows instead of 18 changes membership by nothing and scores slightly worse. On 9 founders the right fund was inside the longer list and it picked it 0 of 9.
Better questions dead A freight-software company was asked plainly and answered “software” — an aviation programme held rank 1 anyway. A robotics company answered “the robots”; nothing moved.
More thinking mixed It does remove the category errors — health grants, farming grants, the cinema fund all gone. But on the only end-to-end metric the no-model control still scored highest at every effort level (16.7% vs 13.9 / 11.1 / 13.9 / 8.3, N=36, nothing separating).
The rows themselves this one 51 of 133 funds on those menus carried no readable sentence saying what they pay for. Zero of the nine it declined had one; 39 of the 54 it chose instead did.

The clearest single row: a scheme reserved for seven southern Italian regions, offered to a university in one of them, rendered to the model as 36 characters of a 725-character description — funds: Manifestazione di interesse (D.D. n. — because our sentence-splitter read D.D. n. as a full stop. The clause naming the founder's own region was cut off before the model saw it. That is now fixed and guarded: 51 unreadable rows became 1, and the build fails if a scope line ever ends mid-phrase again.

Nothing here has been re-measured yet. The account ran out of credit before the run that would test it, and the honest position is that we have fixed what the model reads without yet knowing what that buys.
