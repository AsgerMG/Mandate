§3  THE OUTPUT-LENGTH DISTRIBUTION, FITTED
    Lognormal — positive, right-skewed, long-tailed. The quantile a ceiling has
    to clear (p99.9) sits beyond the largest sample in the tree, so it is
    projected rather than read off.

    wsGA's denominator: 210 API calls (reconstructed: 148 brief(s) + 62 round two), cross-checked
    against its own `thought on 210/148` numerator. Its "4 CUT OFF" is
    therefore 4-in-210 = 1.90%, not 4-in-148 = 2.70%. Reading the
    published line at face value overstates the truncation rate by 42%.

    effort=high   mu=8.741  sigma=0.367   [MLE on 12 exact observations (wsDS bench_high)]
      median 6,257   p95 11,450   p99 14,708   p99.9 19,474   p99.99 24,535
    effort=medium  mu=7.627  sigma=0.529   [mean 2,361 + censoring 4/210 at 6,144 (wsGA)]
      median 2,053   p95 4,898   p99 7,021   p99.9 10,514   p99.99 14,659

    Sanity: the medium fit puts P(output > 6,144) at 1.90% against wsGA's observed 1.90% — it is fitted to that point, so this is a check
    on the solver, not evidence.

    THE TWO FITS DO NOT AGREE ON SPREAD, and that is worth stating rather than
    smoothing: sigma 0.529 at medium against 0.367 at high. They rest on different evidence of
    different quality — the high fit is an MLE on twelve exact observations, the
    medium fit is two summary statistics off one tally line, and a censoring rate
    of 4 events pins a tail loosely. Neither is the safe one to
    assume, so everything below uses the WIDER of the two at each effort, which is
    the direction that cannot flatter the ceiling.

    And the high fit is falsifiable against its own sample: it predicts 3.31%
    of calls above the 12,288 ceiling on n=12 — 0 expected, 0 observed, and the
    largest of the twelve reached 10,596, 86% of it. ██ high has never had headroom; it had luck. ██

§4  THE SCALING NOBODY HAS MEASURED
    Every figure above comes from an 18-row menu. The menu that
    ships is 40. How deliberation scales with the number of rows to be
    compared is UNMEASURED — no paid run has ever been made at forty rows. Three
    scalings bracket it, and the ceiling has to survive the worst of them:

           scaling                                      medium p99.9   high p99.9
    none — length is set by the task, not the menu      10,514     19,474
    log — deliberation grows with ln(rows): x1.28       13,418     24,853
    linear — one unit of thought per row: x2.22         23,364     43,275

    Worst credible case at effort=medium: 23,364 tokens at p99.9.
    The ceiling in force is 12,288. It clears the no-scaling case and fails
    the linear one, which is the case a longer menu is most likely to produce.

§5  WHAT THE CEILING COSTS (nothing) AND WHAT IT RISKS (the owner's money)
    Output tokens bill only when used, so raising a ceiling costs EUR 0 on every
    call that does not need it. The exposure is the calls that do.

    per brief, effort=medium, if a longer menu provokes longer thinking:
      scaling         mean out/call   EUR/call today   EUR/call after 2026-08-31
      none — lengt          2,361           0.0217                     0.0326
      log — delibe          3,013           0.0277                     0.0416
      linear — one          5,246           0.0483                     0.0724

    wsGA billed EUR 9.2660 over 210 API calls (EUR 0.0441/call), of which
    thinking was EUR 4.4937 — 48% of the whole bill.
    Under LINEAR scaling a 1,000-call run costs EUR 71 instead of EUR 44.
    ██ That is the number to put in front of the owner: the ceiling is free, the
    thinking a longer menu may provoke is not. ██

§6  THE CEILING, DERIVED FROM THE ABORT CONDITION RATHER THAN FROM A QUANTILE

    The criterion is not 'a small truncation rate'. wsHK's pre-registration makes
    ANY truncation void the run, so the quantity to bound is

        P(at least one truncation anywhere in the run) = 1 - (1 - p)^N

    A p99.9 ceiling sounds ample and is not: at N API calls it voids the run one
    time in ten. The bar taken here is P(any) < 5% at the worst credible scaling,
    over a run the size of wsGA's (N=210) and over one three times larger.

    effort=medium, LINEAR scaling (the worst case), P(run is voided by truncation):
      ceiling        N=210      N=630     verdict
       12,288       99.8%     100.0%     VOIDS THE RUN
       16,384       80.6%      99.3%     VOIDS THE RUN
       24,576       14.1%      36.5%     VOIDS THE RUN
       32,768        2.0%       5.9%     VOIDS THE RUN
       49,152        0.1%       0.2%     clears the bar

    Smallest ceiling clearing the bar at N=630, linear scaling:
      effort=medium  34,816
      effort=high    57,344   (its distribution sits ~3.0x higher than medium's)

    THE PROPOSED LADDER, and why each rung is the number it is:

      off        1,024   thinking is DISABLED here; the reply is a few dozen tokens
      low        2,048   wsDQ measured max 339 of 1,536 over 12 calls; has never truncated
      medium    34,816   derived above: P(void) < 5% at N=630, linear scaling
      high      57,344   derived above, on high's own twelve exact observations
      xhigh     96,256   no run at any menu; measured step x1.65 continued
      max      128,000   same step, clipped to the model's own 128,000 output cap

    Checked: strictly increasing, and every rung inside the model's 128,000.

    off and low are UNTOUCHED. Raising a ceiling that has never bound buys nothing,
    and widens the worst case on the two rungs where a runaway reply would be least
    expected and least noticed. Thinking is off at one and near-zero at the other.

    THE CONSTRAINT THAT IS NOT max_tokens, and that the record cannot settle:
      wsDS produced a 10,596-token reply inside high's then-150,000ms wall, so
      throughput is AT LEAST 70.6 tok/s. That is a lower bound and cannot be used
      as an upper bound on what the clock can deliver. No recorded run states its
      own duration, so nothing in this tree can say whether 34,816 tokens fit in
      the clock. A ceiling the clock cannot reach does not truncate — it TIMES OUT,
      and a timeout lands in exits.threw where a truncation lands in `truncated`.
      ██ So 'truncated = 0' is not on its own evidence that the ceiling holds. ██
      The fix is not arithmetic: the run must record its own slowest call, and
      `threw` must be a VOID condition beside truncation. Both are in wsHK's
      pre-registration.

    Model ceiling, for completeness: claude-sonnet-5 accepts max_tokens up to
    128,000. Nothing proposed here is anywhere near it; the binding constraint is
    the request budget, not the model.
