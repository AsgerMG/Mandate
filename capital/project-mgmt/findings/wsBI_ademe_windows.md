# Workstream BI — ADEME's windows, and the job that re-reads them

*2026-08-08. Worked on `main` at `f1f4157c`, in a worktree.*

Workstream BE measured the exposure and left it open, correctly, as "a loader
job, not a policy one":

> of the 107 ADEME dispositif pages our rows cite, 102 resolve and **86 state a
> closing date, 38 of them already passed** — while the open-data extract we
> ingest leaves `date_cloture` empty for 97 of 107. That is the single largest
> remaining liveness gap in the French corpus.

France is 31% of cases and the market where the right fund reaches the top five
only 17% of the time, so a French row that has quietly shut costs more than the
same row anywhere else.

---

## What moved

All figures read back **out of Postgres**, with `closed` generated by
`closed_sql()` from the one liveness definition.

| ADEME leaves (75) | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| carrying a window | 46 | **65** |
| closed | 15 | **34** |
| offerable | 60 | **41** |
| no window at all | 29 | 10 |

**19 rows gained a date, and every one of the 19 is in the past** — the earliest
`2022-09-15` (ORPLAST 3), the latest `2025-11-15` (PACTE Entreprises). All 19
were previously NULL, so every one of them was being offered to French founders
as a live fund. France as a whole went from 108 offerable rows to **89**.

Nothing was written that opens a row. Nothing overwrote a date the corpus
already held.

## Why the gap existed at all

`extract_ademe.py` reads `les-aides-financieres-de-l'ademe`, and that file is an
**award register**: 38,669 grant decisions already taken. It has no application
window in it, has never had one, and never will. So every ADEME deadline the
corpus has ever held arrived through a *second* read, of a *catalogue* page —
and 29 of the 75 leaves had never had one taken.

This is the distinction `source_window_policy` was added for, seen from the
other side. The rows are correctly `publishes_window`: ADEME's catalogue does
state closing dates for objects of this kind, so a row of ours carrying none was
a gap in **our** copy. The column said so. Nothing filled it.

## The two catalogues

`agirpourlatransition.ademe.fr` still returns **403** behind Cloudflare on every
path, robots.txt included — re-checked this workstream. That is where a fetcher
stops. The pages are read through `web.archive.org` instead: a public archive of
a public page, no credential presented, no access control circumvented, nothing
passed off as a browser. Had ADEME put those pages behind a login the archive
would not make them ours to take.

| reader | source | rows | what it gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tee` | `mission-transition-ecologique.beta.gouv.fr/api/programs/<slug>` | 49 | `fin de validité`, JSON, 200 |
| `archive` | `agirpourlatransition.ademe.fr/.../aap/<slug>` via Wayback CDX | 21 | the round's `insertDate` window |
| — | `aides-territoires`, `agriculture.gouv.fr` | 5 | no window field on the page; left NULL |

Two properties of those sources decided the design.

**TEE's per-slug endpoint still answers for dispositifs its index has dropped.**
`/api/programs` returns 209 programmes, 57 of them ADEME's, and **none** with a
past `fin de validité` — an aid that ended simply leaves the list. But
`/api/programs/amo-chaufferie-biomasse` still returns, with `fin de validité:
31/12/2024`. Reading the index would have shown a catalogue of nothing but open
aid; reading the slug shows which of ours have shut.

**A pinned Wayback timestamp is a fact that stops being true.** The capture ids
already in `eligibility_source_url` were taken in November 2025; the archive has
re-captured several of those pages since (Innov Eau alone three times, most
recently 2026-06-07). The reader re-resolves to the newest 200 capture on every
run rather than reading the timestamp we stored.

## The rule that decides every write

Four of our leaves are AVELO **rounds** (2, 2.2, 3 1ᵉʳ relevé, 3 2ⁿᵈ relevé) and
three are ORMAT **editions** (2023, 2024, 2025). A programme page states the
programme's window. Handing it to each round dates them all alike — and for
ORMAT that means taking three rows the corpus correctly holds closed
(`2025-06-09`) and asserting from the `ormat` page that they run to `31/12/2026`.
**Reviving a dead call is the exact defect this workstream exists to remove,
arriving through the fix.**

The same separation `liveness.ts` already draws for Vinnova: *the unit is the
object we hold, not the agency*. Operationally, and computable for any page from
any source:

> **exclusive page** — one of our rows cites it. Written, and it overrules what
> we hold. Rule Zero: the source decides.
>
> **shared page** — several of our rows cite it, so the page is the parent of at
> least some of them. It may only FILL a silence: never overwrite a date the row
> already carries, and therefore never move a row from closed to open. If the
> rows already disagree with each other, the page is programme-level for
> certain and the whole group is skipped and reported.

No programme is named anywhere in the code. The discriminator is *how many of
our rows the page has to speak for*, which is a fact about the citation, not
about ADEME.

The AVELO rounds pass through it untouched, because each cites its **own** AAP
page and each therefore gets its own date — 1ᵉʳ relevé `2024-01-15`, 2ⁿᵈ relevé
`2024-07-18`. That is the outcome a per-programme source could not have
produced.

Of the 56 rows not written: 21 already carried exactly what the page says, 25
were held back by the shared-page rule (the three ORMAT editions among them), 5
cite a host with no window field, and 5 cite a page that states no closing date
(AVELO 2 and 2.2 sit on ADEME's older `dispositif-aide` template, which has no
`insertDate` insert at all).

The rule is also what stopped a second, quieter error. Three rows cite the
`vehicules-lourds-electriques` page, which states `07/10/2024`, while the corpus
holds `2024-07-09` for all three — and one of them is the **2023** ecosystem
call, not the 2024 one. Both dates are in the past so no founder was affected,
but writing the page's date onto the 2023 round would have put a claim on the
row that its own source never made.

## What the job does not claim

It writes `scheme_end_date` and `scheme_end_date_basis` and nothing else. The
basis is always `call_deadline`: an appel à projets clôture and a `fin de
validité` are both this instrument's own application window, and past means
closed permanently. It is never `notified_duration`, which is a State-aid
notification expiry — routinely prolonged, and not a thing ADEME publishes.

The three `eligibility_*` columns are left exactly as the matcher set them. They
describe the read that produced `eligibility_text`, and this job read a date, not
a text: repointing the citation at whichever Wayback capture this run used would
assert the stored text came off that capture, bumping `_fetched_at` would assert
the text was re-confirmed, and `_confidence` grades the MATCH between our leaf
and that page, which reading a date off it tells you nothing about. The window's
own provenance — page, field, capture date — goes to
`data/raw/ademe_windows/readings.json`, and every run re-derives it.

## What re-runs it

`ETL/terms/fetch_ademe_windows.py --write`, as a step in `railway_cron.py`,
immediately after `refresh_open_calls.py` and for the same reason. Spain's
convocatorias were the first source in the corpus that decays on a **calendar**
rather than on a release; ADEME is the second, and the failure mode is identical
— nothing upstream changes when a call shuts, so `renew.py`'s content-hash
trigger never fires and the corpus goes on offering it. `scheme_end_date` is
also rebuilt from the extractors on every load, so a hand `UPDATE` survives
until the next rebuild and no longer.

The step is idempotent, re-reads both catalogues from scratch, exits 3 on a
partial read (declared in `PARTIAL_OK`, the archive 503s and times out
routinely), and carries a 25-minute wall-clock ceiling so it can never hold up
the census behind it. Cheap source first: TEE's 49 rows finish in about a
minute, so a truncated tail is always the expensive archive rows, which the next
run retries.

### The guard that catches the job's own absence

`check_offerable_decay.py` limb 1 exists so that a weekly job which silently
stops is caught before a single row expires. Its docstring promises the property
outright: *"no source is named here, no country is named here, and a second
register that starts publishing deadlines is covered on the day it lands."*

It was not. The limb joined `funding_instrument_sources` INNER, that table maps
only the ADEME parent regime (one row, `ademe_aid` → `ademe_aid`), and the
parent carries no end date — so **46 French call deadlines, the largest
clock-decaying population outside Spain, were watched by nothing at all.** The
join is now LEFT with a fall-back to `root_id`, which is the unit a refresh job
runs on anyway. Coverage went from 1 watched source to 4:

```
ademe_aid                       46 rows
bdns_es_ayudas                  82 rows     <- also previously unwatched
bdns_es_convocatorias_abiertas  88 rows
fr_relance_industrie             1 rows
```

The run bumps `updated_at` on every row it **confirmed**, not only the ones whose
date moved — otherwise a correctly quiet quarter and a job that stopped running
look the same from inside the guard, which is the confusion it exists for. The
second run of the day proved that end to end: `read 70, 0 window(s) to write,
confirmed against source: 70 row(s) touched`. Idempotent, and still visibly
alive.

The offerable baseline is re-cut here, deliberately: France losing 29 offerable
rows is 25% against a 15% tolerance, and the guard correctly refused it as decay
until told otherwise. The fall is the point of the workstream — those rows are
funds that had shut.

## The write that would have reached nobody

Postgres is the corpus, but it is not what a founder reads. `website/build/
eligibility_export.py` builds `public/data/instrument_terms.json` — the
committed snapshot the site serves and every fallback path loads — **out of
SQLite**. The first version of this job copied only the rows it had *changed*
into the cache, which is the obvious thing and the wrong one:

```
postgres:  65 ADEME windows        sqlite:  0
```

Zero. Not 46 and not 19 — the cache had never held an ADEME window at all,
including the 46 that predate this workstream. Every date would have landed in
the corpus and none of them in the product. The job now copies the **state** of
the root rather than the change, which repairs a store that drifted for any
reason, and `check_corpus_currency.ts` went from listing fifteen ADEME rows as
`postgres CLOSED / snapshot open` to:

> the snapshot and Postgres agree on every liveness verdict

The snapshot is patched here for exactly those 19 rows' `schemeEndDate` and
`schemeEndBasis` — 38 lines. A full re-export was run first and discarded: it
also carried 400 rows of gate changes, 87 `administeringBody` edits and 41
`sourceWindowPolicy` values sitting in the shared cache from other workstreams,
none of which are this branch's to ship.

`check_hydration_parity.ts` still fails, on those same gate differences. It was
already failing before this workstream (verdict of 2026-08-08T22:10Z) and none
of the differences it names is a date.

## Two things found on the way, not fixed here

**`data/reference/source_window_policy.json` does not exist.** `data` is
blanket-gitignored, the file was never force-added, and the worktree it was
written in has been removed. `assign_source_window_policy.py` therefore fails
with `FileNotFoundError` on any machine, and the `source_window_policy` column
survives only as values already in Postgres — including the `closed_on`
declaration that closes France Relance. The mechanism is sound and the evidence
behind it is gone. This is the second time this exact trap has been sprung;
`check_offerable_decay.py` documents surviving it once already.

**Five ADEME leaves cite pages with no window field**, and two of them cite
`gestion-des-biodechets`, whose `opérateur de contact` is *Chambre de Commerce
et de l'Industrie* rather than ADEME. Those are match-quality questions for the
matcher that wrote them, not window questions; the reader records the operator
it saw and writes nothing.
