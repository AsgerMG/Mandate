## Bottom line

- You have one traced public award, €1.0M in state aid from development banks in 2021. No comparable companies in your sub-sector are traced in the database, so there is no peer pattern to benchmark against.

- Bpifrance inov is the right first move: it is a national grant and soft-loan line open to French SMEs, with no documented rule closing you out.

- PACTE Industrie is open to you on paper, but ADEME's incentive-effect rule requires the aid application to precede the start of work, and you told us work has already started on this project. That closes this route for the current phase of work unless you scope a new, not-yet-started phase.

- The three French state aid regime rows (via Conseil régional, Conseil régional Grand Est) sit alongside Bpifrance as national doors: each is a separate legal route with its own typical award, worth checking in parallel rather than choosing just one.

- A national R&D grant under these regimes is non-dilutive money you keep in full, unlike the EIC's blended option which takes an equity stake through the EIC Fund.


## Positioning

Frame this project against the industrial-modernisation priority the French state and regions just reinforced: Bpifrance and the regions launched a fifth, regionalised axis of France 2030 in July 2026 dedicated to helping industrial SMEs adopt advanced manufacturing technologies, alongside the existing innovation axis, a fit for a company adding a new surface-treatment step to additive manufacturing's value chain. Bpifrance and the regional authorities behind your state-aid rows score projects on innovation and regional industrial impact rather than on a rubric of public purpose or market excellence alone, so the case to make is that this deposition technology extends what French industrial equipment and materials suppliers can already produce, not a pitch about end users you do not sell to. Horizon EU CL4's own record leans toward semiconductors and electronics, which matches the materials and process work you do, and is worth citing as fit if you build a consortium. Keep the frame on the technology's place in the additive-manufacturing supply chain and the equipment makers and materials suppliers who would adopt it, since that is the only commercial relationship you have described.

## State of play

Horizon EU CL4 sits inside a live redesign: the Commission's July 2025 proposal for a €234 billion European Competitiveness Fund would fold Horizon Europe's Pillar II into four new windows from 2028, with a €51.5 billion digital-leadership window and a €125.2 billion resilience-and-industry window covering territory close to your work. The scope and allocation of those windows are still being negotiated with the Council and Parliament as of July 2026, so a CL4 proposal built on today's call topics may need reframing once the new windows are set. EIC Accelerator has also just opened a distinct €100M defence-focused call in June 2026, separate from its standard Accelerator track, a sign the programme is segmenting by strategic priority rather than holding one uniform call. Neither of these changes your eligibility today, but both mean the argument that wins a Horizon or EIC application in 2026 may not be the argument that wins after 2028.
