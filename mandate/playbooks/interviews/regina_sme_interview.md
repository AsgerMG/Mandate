# Regina: subject-matter-expert interview guide

For the interview with an EU policy / regulatory affairs expert (11 Nov 2026). The goal
is not facts (everything factual is public and Regina researches it live). The goal
is the expert's **instinct**: how a senior reads a file, what they check first, where
juniors go wrong, when they say "don't do this yet", and what great looks like. Output:
`mandate/playbooks/policy-*.md` (one per job type), 4-6 eval cases with the expert's model
answers, and corrections to `regina.md`.

Same method as Wincent's interview (`funding-routes.md`): mentality, not encyclopedia;
examples are feelers for the procedure; every habit written so it works for any file,
any country.

---

## How Claude runs it (live)

Trigger: the owner says **"let's do the interview for Regina"**.

1. **Open (2 min).** Introduce the purpose in two sentences; confirm the expert is fine
   with the conversation being transcribed and turned into the agent's playbook (public
   professional knowledge only; no client names or confidential matters: anonymise
   cases). Ask how they would like to be credited (or not).
2. **One question at a time.** Ask, then wait. Short follow-ups that make it concrete:
   "walk me through the last time that happened", "what did you check first", "what
   would a junior have done", "how did you know".
3. **Mirror every 10-15 minutes.** Read back in 3-4 lines what was heard as *habits*
   ("so the rule is: before engaging the Commission, you check where the file sits in
   the Commission's own planning, because...") and ask the expert to correct it. The
   corrected version is what goes into the playbook.
4. **Keep the expert's words.** Capture verbatim phrases that carry instinct; they
   become the playbook's voice and the eval's must/must-not.
5. **Never lead.** Do not propose the answer; ask, then probe.
6. **Time-box.** ~90 min. If time runs short, protect modules 2, 3 and 7 (method,
   the case walkthroughs, the grading exercise).
7. **Close.** Read back the 5-8 habits that came out strongest; ask "what did I not
   ask that a junior would most need to hear?"; ask permission to follow up with 2-3
   questions in writing.
8. **Afterwards (same day):** write `mandate/playbooks/policy-*.md`, the eval cases with
   model answers, the corrections to `regina.md`; commit; run Regina on one case on the
   bench and send the expert the result to grade (round 2 by grading, as with Wincent).

## Module 0. Who they are (5 min)
- What do you work on, for whom (in general terms), and for how long?
- Which institutions and files do you know best? Where are you weakest?
- If you had to train a strong junior in one month, what would week one be?

## Module 1. The four job types: which matter, which come first (10 min)
Regina's planned job types: **regulatory & enforcement watch; consultation responses
and the legislative process; positioning & media; coalitions & stakeholders.**
- Which of these do clients actually pay for? Which is most of your week?
- What is missing from this list? (e.g. crisis/rapid reaction, standards bodies,
  national transposition, public procurement, trade/sanctions)
- Which one would you trust an AI with first, and which never?

## Module 2. The method: how a senior reads a file (20 min)
Core: *"A client calls: 'There is an EU file that could hurt us.' What do you do in the
first hour, the first day, the first week?"*
Follow-ups:
- What do you establish before anything else? (the file's stage; the procedure; the
  lead DG/unit and the cabinet; Council presidency priorities; EP committee,
  rapporteur and shadows; national positions; the impact assessment; the timeline)
- How do you tell where the real decision is being made, and by whom?
- How do you judge what is still movable and what is already locked? (Commission
  proposal vs EP report vs Council general approach vs trilogue)
- Delegated and implementing acts, guidelines, standards: when is that the real
  battlefield rather than the legislation?
- National level: when do you go through a member state (perm rep, ministry) rather
  than Brussels? How do you choose which capitals?
- Enforcement (Commission/DG COMP, national regulators, DMA/DSA/AI Act bodies): how does
  watching an enforcement case differ from a legislative file? What signals matter?
- **Sequencing:** what must be done before engaging (transparency register, the
  position paper, the coalition, the data)? What is the classic "too early / too late"?
- **"Don't do this yet":** when do you advise a client NOT to act? How do you know?

## Module 3. Walk through real-shaped cases (20 min)
Ask the expert to work each aloud, as they would for a client. Probe every step:
"why that first", "who exactly", "what would you send".
1. **Travel scale-up following antitrust/DMA enforcement against Google.** What does the
   client need to know, follow and do? Cases and proceedings, how to get updates,
   coalitions, media, how to position, the angle that is grounded and politically
   appropriate.
2. **AI-native learning product for young children** entering the EU market: the AI
   Act (education, minors), GDPR for children's data, DSA protection of minors. What
   are the deal-breakers, what is the sequencing, who do they engage?
3. **Trust-AI startup (EUR 2M raised) positioning as "Europe's safe alternative".**
   Which policy hooks make that credible; which media and journalists; which
   associations; what would the expert never claim?
4. *(If time)* A file the expert worked recently (anonymised): what happened, what they
   got right, what they would do differently.

## Module 4. People and institutions (10 min)
- In the Commission: DG, director, head of unit, desk officer, cabinet: who is P1 for a
  file and who is P2? When do you go to the cabinet vs the unit?
- In Parliament: rapporteur, shadows, committee secretariat, political group advisers,
  APAs: who actually shapes the text?
- In Council: presidency, working party, attachés, perm reps: when do they matter most?
- National: ministries, regulators, parliamentary committees.
- How do you find the right person when it is not obvious? How do you keep that map
  current?
- Political vs operational stakeholders: the same split Wincent uses: does it hold?

## Module 5. Positions, consultations, outreach (10 min)
- What makes a consultation response read rather than counted? Length, structure,
  evidence, tone?
- A position paper that lands: what is in it, what is never in it?
- Meeting requests: what gets a yes from a head of unit or an MEP's office? Show an
  example opener (formal, local, strategic, short: the paper-stack test).
- Media: when does media help a file and when does it hurt? How do you pick outlets and
  journalists?
- Coalitions: when do you join, build or stay solo? What does a coalition cost the
  client?

## Module 6. Monitoring and instinct (10 min)
- Which sources do you read every day, every week? (official journals, agendas, EP
  committee calendars, Council agendas, Agence Europe/MLex/Politico Pro, regulator
  pages, social media)
- What is the signal that something is about to move? What is noise?
- How do you decide a development is worth calling a client about today?
  (This is the rule JEV/the radar must learn.)
- Precedent: how do you use past files, past enforcement, past positions of an
  official?
- Where do juniors go wrong? (the three most common mistakes)

## Module 7. Grading exercise (10 min)
Show the expert one Regina answer produced on the bench before the interview (case 1 or
2 above). Ask:
- Grade 1-5: would you send it to a client?
- What is wrong, missing, naive, or politically off?
- Rewrite the first paragraph the way you would say it.
The critique becomes round 2 of the playbook; the rewrite becomes the model answer.
