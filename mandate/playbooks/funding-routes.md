# Playbook: funding and finance routes

Owner interview, rounds 1-2 (2026-11-09). This is how a seasoned public-affairs
practitioner works a public-funding or public-finance case. The agent follows
it; it does not improvise around it.

**A mentality, not an encyclopedia.** The owner's examples are feelers for the
procedure, not a list to memorise; everything factual is public and the agent
researches it live, cited. What cannot be looked up is the judgement below. The owner's examples teach the method,
not the complete list of vehicles. The agent applies the method and RESEARCHES
the vehicles (corpus first, then live research, always cited). It is judged on
reasoning, sequencing and research, not on recalling a particular fund. Status: DRAFT; round 3 (owner's grading of the fintech case, 10 Nov) applied.

## 1. Before any instrument: establish the structure
Never name an instrument before these are known (ask only what the company
profile lacks):
- **Legal structure:** legal form, where incorporated, where it has branches,
  subsidiaries or establishments, and in which markets it operates or wants to.
  Eligibility is decided by structure first: a fund open to "an SME established
  in Bavaria" is closed to a company with no Bavarian establishment, however
  good the project.
- **Regulatory status:** licences held or not held (banking, payment,
  investment firm, intermediary registrations). See the deal-breakers in §4.
- **Alignment:** how the company's activity fits the fund's goals and the
  relevant industrial agenda (national, regional, EU). Alignment is not only an
  eligibility test, it is a **goodwill lever**: a case framed in the
  institution's own priorities is read differently.
- **Documents:** what each vehicle's process will demand. Preparing them is part
  of the advice, not an afterthought.

## 1b. When the founder does not know what to ask for: diagnose intent
Often the founder only knows "help us with public money". Then the first job
is a short diagnostic, not a list of instruments. Ask, in plain words:
- **Are you raising, now or soon?** If yes, **matchmaking / co-investment
  loans** are the classic first route: the state matches or doubles private
  money (often venture debt on favourable terms) because the private
  investors' due diligence is already done, so the state backs the startup
  without doing that work itself. A round in progress is the moment.
- **Do you do real R&D?** Then R&D grants and subsidised R&D programmes.
- **Have you looked at R&D tax credits?** (country-specific; research and cite).
- **Are you entering a new market?** Then export-credit and market-entry
  support, with the sequencing rule (apply before you enter).
- **What are your ambitions and constraints?** Hiring, plants, partnerships,
  public customers.
Then sequence: what has to happen first for the others to stay possible.

**Converge with Regina when the activity is regulated.** Example: AI-native
learning products for young children touch the AI Act (education and minors),
children's data under GDPR, and the DSA's protection of minors. These shape
both what funders will back and how the company positions itself; flag them
in the same answer, not later.

## 1c. Precedent is the strongest argument
For every route, find **precedents**: the same fund or institution backing a
company in the same situation. "You guaranteed an alternative lender's loan
portfolio before", "you matched a round of the same size for a startup at our
stage with 1x-2x venture debt", "you gave export credits to a tech company
expanding the way we are". It need not be a competitor, only the same
situation: same instrument, similar stage, size, raise, sector or move.
- Precedents prove the route is real, set realistic amounts and terms, and
  give the institution a reason to say yes (they have done it before).
- Use them in outreach: the strongest line to a head of guarantees is the deal
  they already did.
- Source: the capital brain's award records (who received what, from which
  instrument, when, how much, each with its source), then the institution's
  own published transaction lists, then live research. Always cited.
- Say when there is no precedent: that is itself information (a first-of-a-
  kind ask needs a different, stronger case).

## 2. The working order
1. **Map the structure and gather the foundational facts** (§1). Advance
   informed or not at all.
2. **Map the institutions** in every market the company wants to reach, at
   every level that matters: **regional, local, national, EU**.
3. **Within each institution, rank the people:** priority-1 and priority-2
   contacts, i.e. those you need to win over to create goodwill.
4. **Targeted outreach** (§3).

## 2b. Two kinds of stakeholder, and who is priority 1
Public financing always has both:
- **Political stakeholders** move agendas, forward you to the right people and
  put you on top of the pile. They open doors; they do not sign.
- **Public-capital stakeholders** are operational: the people who grant,
  guarantee, invest and sign off.
Both matter; work them together.

**Priority 1** are the people responsible for the matter, high enough that
landing on their desk produces a top-down hand-off: directors-general, heads
of unit, and the head of the specific instrument or line (for a portfolio
guarantee: head of guarantees / microfinance / alternative lending). Not the
CEO or the board of a KfW: one level down from the very top.
**Priority 2** are the next level down: heads of the specific division or
instrument a P1 would forward you to.
- If P1 does not answer, go down to the heads of unit.
- With a genuine personal contact, you can escalate to the board.
- A top-down introduction ("the director asked me to look at this") is worth
  more than any cold approach lower down.

## 3. Outreach that gets read
- **Research the person first:** who they are, where they have been, what they
  care about, which regional or ministerial agenda they carry. Tailor every
  message to that agenda.
- **Reach them in the right function.** The same person can hold several roles
  (a Bundestag member who is also a ministry official). Write to the role that
  decides your matter, through that role's channel, in that role's language.
  Writing to the wrong function is a classic junior mistake.
- **Formal, local, strategic.** Formal register, local context, respect for the
  position. Senior people (P1, P2) speak strategy, not operations: the case is
  "this is a strategic priority for you", and eligibility through structure
  is justified *later*. Example opener for Germany: the CEO is German, the
  company is a European champion aligned with industrial robotics deployment,
  and it helps the Mittelstand get the financing it needs to modernise and
  revive the German industrial agenda. Speak the language they want to hear.
- **No AI voice.** Nothing weak, vague or long. Cut to the bone and show you
  know the field, without operational detail.
- **Bring something to the table.** Introduce the company in two lines, give
  just enough context, state why it serves *their* priority, and ask for a
  meeting to discuss alignment and opportunities (e.g. regional priorities,
  collaboration).
- **Short and concise, always.** The paper-stack test: picture a German
  Landtag or Bundestag member who finds a stack of copied papers on the desk
  every morning and allocates them through the day to the right ministry or
  head of unit. Your message must stand out on that stack and be routable in
  one read. Institutions work old-school; write for that.

## 3b. Knowing the agenda: be present on every channel
Being informed is the alpha and omega. Read every channel an institution or
region speaks through: media, social media, newsletters, press releases,
speeches, strategies. Example: before approaching Bavaria on robotics, know
Bavaria's robotics strategy. The agent maintains this per institution and
region, and the radar watches it.

## 4. Deal-breakers: where institutional flexibility is near zero
Scan for these before recommending anything. They are formal; no amount of
goodwill moves them.
- **Formal eligibility of the applicant type.** Example, guarantees for a
  lender's loan portfolio:
  - **Germany:** the state guarantees SME lending through the normal banks
    (the **Hausbank** principle, KfW and the Bürgschaftsbanken). **Without a
    banking licence there is no route today** (owner, round 3): do not dress
    up a partner-bank workaround as the answer. The route is POLITICAL: a
    reform is moving in parliament, so the move is to get in the room with the
    federal economy ministry (BMWE) heads of department who host that
    conversation, and help it accelerate (§4d). Research and cite the reform's
    current state before advising.
  - **Italy:** more open: it will guarantee portfolios. Authorised
    financial-intermediary status under **Art. 106 TUB** is the unlock; with
    it, the national schemes (Fondo di Garanzia per le PMI) are straightforward.
    **Recommend getting it** (sequenced), do not park the market for want of
    it. A fund-only structure (e.g. a Luxembourg RAIF) does not give
    streamlined access on its own.
  - **France:** different mechanics. NOT asserted from instinct: the agent
    must research and cite the formal documents (Bpifrance guarantee
    products, Code monétaire et financier) before advising.
  - **EU / InvestEU (EIF):** more open to alternative lenders as financial
    intermediaries, and for a lender operating across several member states
    **the quickest route of all** (owner, round 3): skip the regional and
    federal agencies and go to the EU. Match the product to the activity:
    the InvestEU **digitalisation** or **SME competitiveness** guarantee
    products (robot deployment fits digitalisation). **Watch:** InvestEU is moving under the European
    Competitiveness Fund; check whether alternative lenders stay eligible
    (carve-outs) as the rules are rewritten.
- **Sequencing.** A mentality, not a list: *before every move, ask what must
  already be done for the next step to remain possible.* Examples: a
  lobby/transparency-register entry before engaging officials; export-credit
  or market-entry support usually requires the application (and the budget)
  to be filed **before** entering the market; starting work before applying
  can void an aid's incentive effect. Never be in a situation where a
  requirement was not anticipated.
- **Processes.** Formal channels, portals, deadlines, required documents.

## 4b. Keep a finger on the pulse
Rules change under you: programmes merge, mandates move, carve-outs appear or
vanish (InvestEU into the European Competitiveness Fund is the live example).
Every route the agent recommends is also put on the workspace watchlist, so a
rule change that reopens or closes it is flagged.

## 4c. Prioritise, probe, park; rarely walk away
- **Rarely cut ties.** Only an explicit, hard no ends a route; otherwise park
  it and monitor whether the rules change.
- **Spend energy where the return is highest.** Do not pursue every route
  equally. Pick a few public routes to run **in parallel**, because public
  processes are slow (unlike a VC partner putting EUR 200k on the table).
- **Low-effort probes are worth it.** A short, very specific message to the
  head of guarantees or investments at a public institution, asking what the
  options are, costs little. If they cannot accommodate the need: park it,
  monitor, move on.

## 4d. Choosing the route: three habits of a senior (owner, round 3)
Facts change; these do not. Apply them to every case, every country.
1. **Pick the level with the open door.** EU, national, regional: the same
   need is often open at one level and shut at another for this applicant
   type. Name the level that is open for THIS applicant and lead with it;
   do not work every level in parallel out of thoroughness.
2. **When a status unlocks a market, recommend getting the status.** A
   licence, registration or authorisation that opens the door (Art. 106 TUB
   in Italy) is a plan step with a sequence and a timeline, not a reason to
   park the market.
3. **When the formal door is shut, the route is changing the rule.** If a
   reform is moving, find it, the ministry that hosts it and the heads of
   department who can raise it to the political level, and help accelerate
   it. If nothing is moving, **the change becomes the company's lobby aim**:
   name the rule that blocks (the exact clause), the unit that owns it, the
   next moment it can change (a renewal, a budget, a review), engage that
   unit, and put it on the watchlist. Say plainly that there is no route
   today. (Evidence, round 3: Germany's counter-guarantee for the
   Bürgschaftsbanken admits only banks, building societies and insurers; it
   is renegotiated for 2028; the economy ministry's SME-finance unit is the
   door.)

## 4e. Go below the national level, with a local read (owner, round 3b)
"National" is three levels: national/federal, regional, local. A senior works
all three, and narrows them twice:
1. **By status.** Once the company holds a status (Art. 106 TUB, startup
   innovativa, JEI, an establishment in a Land), list the products that status
   opens, at each level, and lead with those. Status is matched, not guessed.
2. **By economic geography.** The activity decides which regions matter, and
   their priorities make the case. Example: robot deployment in Italy points to
   the industrial north (Lombardia, Veneto, Piemonte, Emilia-Romagna, Toscana)
   and to Lazio (defence, the capital). Ground the read in the regions' own
   strategies (Smart Specialisation, ERDF programme priorities) and cite them.
3. **Keep the political read current.** Regional councils and giunte, ministry
   decrees, budget laws and reforms change what is open; the watchlist follows
   the regions and ministries the plan relies on.

## 4f. Follow the money through its channels, on both sides (PROVISIONAL, round 5)
Proposed by the main session from the forklift case (12 Nov); awaiting the owner's
confirmation. Remove this note when confirmed, or delete the section if rejected.
1. **Public money often reaches a company through an intermediary.** A guarantee
   fund backs banks and leasing companies; a fund-of-funds backs VC funds; an export
   agency covers the customer's bank. When no direct route opens, ask which of the
   client's own banks, lessors and investors already carry public backing, and route
   through them. "None found" for a fund-of-funds means: find the funds it backs.
2. **Look at both sides of the deal.** What lowers the CUSTOMER's cost (their
   investment grants, tax depreciation, buyer financing, prevention premiums) can
   speed sales and payment more than anything that lowers the company's own cost of
   capital. Name the customer-side levers in every deployment or sales-cycle case.
3. **Bring in the regulatory side when it moves demand.** A safety or compliance
   rule that the product helps customers meet is a sales argument and often a grant
   hook: ask Regina (converge, §1b).

## 5. Where juniors go wrong
- The **preparatory steps**: not asking the right questions up front, so the
  real blockers surface late.
- **Outreach to the wrong function** (§3).
- Messages that are too long, without context, or that bring nothing.
