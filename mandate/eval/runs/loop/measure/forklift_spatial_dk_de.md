## Wincent's answer

## Answer

**Working capital:** go to Denmark first. Your ApS signs the German contracts and builds the hardware in Denmark, so every German deployment counts as a Danish export. That gives you EIFO, Denmark's state export and investment fund. It can guarantee part of a bank's loss on a new line for your project float [c5][c1]. It can also insure the payment you wait for at go-live [c14][c11]. The cost stays with you, the risk moves to the state, and your capital cycles from project to project faster (judgement).

**Equity:** yes, two public co-investors fit a round with a lead already in talks. One is EIFO's own equity arm in Denmark [w2]. The other is Wachstumsfonds Bayern 2, if it accepts your Bavarian subsidiary as the qualifying presence [w3]. Four months is just enough if you open both through your lead now (judgement).

## Why this is the cheap money

Your problem is a mispriced risk. A bank cannot easily price a receivable that pays only at go-live, so it charges a lot or lends little (judgement). Fixing that kind of gap is exactly what EIFO's guarantees are for. Pitch it as their mission meeting a mispriced market, backed by your pipeline.

| Money | Carries | Why cheap or dear |
|---|---|---|
| Bank line with EIFO guarantee | Hardware and installation float | Cheap. EIFO covers up to 80% of the bank's loss on the facility, capped at 50% of its total loss on you [c1]. The premium is 0.8% to 6.3% a year [c4]. |
| EIFO Contract Guarantee | Risk that a customer does not pay at go-live | Cheap risk transfer. Covers up to 90% of your loss [c11], if EIFO accepts the buyer [c10]. |
| Series A equity | Product, sales, team | The dearest money: dilution plus venture target returns. Do not spend it on hardware float (judgement). |

**A worked example on assumed numbers.** Say each deployment ties up EUR 200k for 5 months. Then EUR 2.4M of equity covers about 12 projects at once, roughly 29 a year. A EUR 4M guaranteed line covers about 20 at once, roughly 48 a year, for interest plus the premium (judgement). Please send your real average upfront cost per project and the months from order to go-live.

Can two EIFO products sit on the same order? I could not confirm that. Ask EIFO (unverified).

## What not to do

- **Don't move the cost onto customers.** EIFO's Buyer Financing exists (corpus row, uncited), but it makes your offer less competitive. Use it later as a sales aid, never as the fix.
- **Don't push German contracts into the GmbH to fit a Bavarian loan.** Those orders would probably stop counting as Danish exports (judgement).
- **Don't ask EIFO to guarantee your existing lines.** The financing must expand the bank's commitment [c9]. Ask for a new or larger line.

## Germany and EU

- **Bavaria, loans:** I could not confirm this from the official page. LfA's Universalkredit reportedly funds working capital for firms with a Bavarian establishment, via your Hausbank, with optional risk relief for the bank (unverified). It only helps once the GmbH itself carries costs. Parked.
- **EU:** ask your Danish bank whether it already has EU guarantee backing from the EIF that could sit on your line (unverified). It is a cheap probe.

## Equity in the round

- **EIFO equity.** It invests as an active minority investor, in partnership with co-investors [w2]. It already holds stakes in Danish hardware firms like NIL Technology ApS (register record: https://eifo.dk/for-dig-som-er/fond-eller-investor/investering-i-virksomheder).
- **Wachstumsfonds Bayern 2.** It invests EUR 2M to 8M, never as lead [w3]. A private investor must come in on the same terms and for the same amount, and the application goes in with that investor's statement [w3]. It requires an SME corporation with its seat or an establishment in Bavaria [w3]. Your GmbH is a subsidiary, not an establishment of the ApS. Whether it qualifies is the one question to put to Bayern Kapital.
- **After close:** EIFO's venture-debt loan for companies that have raised venture equity (unverified). The Match Loan looks closed to you, because its revenue cap is below yours (unverified).
