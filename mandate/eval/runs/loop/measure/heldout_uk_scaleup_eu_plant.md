## The case

> We are a UK climate-tech scale-up, 80 people, making heat-pump components in Sheffield. We want to open a second plant in the EU in 2027, probably Germany or northern Italy, and sell across the EU. Which public money supports the plant and the entry, and should the location decide it?
> 
> More about us, so you can answer in one go: UK Ltd, EUR 14M revenue, loss-making, backed by two VCs; no EU subsidiary yet. The plant would cost about EUR 25M, with 60 jobs in year one; we have not chosen the site and have not signed anything. We also want UK support for the export side.
>

## Wincent's answer

I'd treat the choice of country and site as a commercial decision and let the aid decide between the sites that pass. It's not quite "the location decides the money". The location decides what kind of public money you get. In Germany it is a grant from the state government (the Land), and only on sites in a designated aid area. In Italy it is a national soft loan, which barely depends on the region. The EU grant doesn't care about the site at all. You are loss-making, so a large cheap loan may be worth more to you than a grant of 10 to 20% (judgement). That changes the Germany versus Italy comparison more than the aid percentages do.

## Where you stand

**EU: probe.** The Innovation Fund's last clean-tech call funded "the manufacturing of components for … heat pumps", for projects with capex above €2.5M [w3]. The project must be located in the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway [w9]. That call closed on 23 April 2026 [w3]. One advisory firm expects the next round to open in December 2026 and close in April 2027 (unverified; confirm on the EU funding portal) [w10]. It is competitive, but the timing fits a 2027 plant (judgement).

**Germany: open, but only on certain sites.** The regional investment grant (GRW) takes small, medium and large firms [c7]. In Lower Saxony, for example, the site must be inside the listed aid areas [w2], and the project must create permanent jobs and contribute to environmental protection [c8]. Assuming the grant is 20% for a medium-sized firm and 10% for a large one (an uncited figure in my sources for Bavaria; unverified), €25M of capex gives a €2.5M to €5M grant. A federal clean-tech scheme for net-zero technologies is still waiting for EU approval [w5]. On a site outside the aid areas the grant mostly disappears (judgement).

**Italy: open in the north through a national route.** Invitalia's development contracts take companies "italiane o estere, di qualsiasi dimensione" (Italian or foreign, of any size) [w1] [c1], for large industrial investments [c2]. The floor is €20M [w1], and your €25M plant clears it. Support includes a subsidised loan of up to 75% of eligible costs, roughly €18.75M on your plant if all of it is eligible [w1]. The net-zero window closed on 21 April 2026 [w1], so it has to reopen. The regional aid map covers only parts of Lombardy, Veneto and Emilia-Romagna [w7]. Once you have an Italian site and are confirmed as an SME, you can also use the national SME guarantee fund [c15] and the Nuova Sabatini machinery loan scheme [c16]. All four of those regions' innovation strategies name both advanced manufacturing and energy [c18]-[c23].

**UK: open for the Sheffield export side.** UK Export Finance's General Export Facility needs at least 20% of turnover from UK exports in one of the last three years, or 5% in each [w4]. Your goods must be made in the UK, and facilities go up to about £25M [w4]. It backs working capital for Sheffield, not the EU plant (judgement).

## Flags
1. **High: don't sign anything yet.** In Germany you apply "vor Beginn des Projekts" (before the project starts) [w2]. The same applies to any aid (unverified for Italy). Equipment orders, building contracts and binding leases come after you file.
2. **High: the applicant must be the entity that invests** [c6]. Set up the German GmbH or Italian S.r.l. before you file.
3. **High: your SME status is unproven.** It depends on your two VCs' stakes [c17]. If you count as large, the German grant halves.
4. **Medium: the "company in difficulty" test.** Losses above half of your subscribed capital bar you from most aid (uncited rule). Test this on your accounts now.
5. **For Regina:** EU–UK rules of origin for Sheffield parts going into the EU plant, and refrigerant rules shaping demand for your parts.

## Decisions
- **Location.** Pick a shortlist of three or four sites on customers, suppliers and labour. Then rank them by aid-area status and the Italian loan (judgement).
- **Grant or loan.** A €5M grant in Germany versus a soft loan of up to €18.75M in Italy. For a loss-making company I lean towards the loan, if Italy is commercially equal (judgement).

## Plan
- **This month:** run the SME and difficulty tests. Agree the shortlist. Make first contact with the German state development bank (the Landesbank) and with Invitalia.
- **Quarter 1 2027:** incorporate the EU company. File the aid applications. Start the Innovation Fund application.
- **Before you sign any contract:** written confirmation from the German bank [w2], or an accepted Italian application.
- **Run in parallel:** the export facility through your UK bank [w4].

**Who to approach (by role):**
- **Germany:** the Land economy ministry's head of regional policy (decides), and the business-development office of the town where the site is (forwards).
- **Italy:** Invitalia's head of development contracts, and the regional economy minister's office.
- **EU:** the Innovation Fund team at the EU agency that runs it (CINEA).
- **UK:** UK Export Finance's Yorkshire export finance manager, through your bank.

**Draft (send in German):**
> Dear Head of Regional Policy, we make heat-pump components in Sheffield (80 people, two VC investors) and will build our first EU plant in 2027: €25M and 60 jobs in year one. [Region] is on our shortlist because your strategy puts clean-tech manufacturing at its centre. May we discuss your priorities for sites like this in a short meeting? Nothing is signed yet.

**Precedents:**
- **Italy:** Invitalia's 2026 awards to Metinvest Adria S.p.A., €15M to €30M per tranche (register record, [SA.118553](https://competition-cases.ec.europa.eu/cases/SA.118553)).
- **Germany:** Lower Saxony grants to small manufacturers, for example Poly Plast GmbH at €792k (register record, [SA.112200](https://competition-cases.ec.europa.eu/cases/SA.112200)). I found no heat-pump plant.
- **Innovation Fund and UK export facility:** none found.

**Watch:**
- The EU regional aid maps end on 31 December 2027, and new ones apply from January 2028 [w6]. If your decision slips into 2028, a site's status can change.
- Italy's net-zero window reopening [w1].
- EU approval of the German clean-tech scheme [w5].
- The next Innovation Fund call [w10].

**Questions for you:**
- What are the VCs' stakes and voting rights? This decides your SME status and therefore the aid rate.
- How does your capex split between building and machinery? This sets what is eligible.
- Over the last three years, what share of turnover came from exports? This decides the UK facility.
- Where are your main heat-pump customers? This is what should pick the shortlist.
