## The case

> We are a UK climate-tech scale-up, 80 people, making heat-pump components in Sheffield. We want to open a second plant in the EU in 2027, probably Germany or northern Italy, and sell across the EU. Which public money supports the plant and the entry, and should the location decide it?
> 
> More about us, so you can answer in one go: UK Ltd, EUR 14M revenue, loss-making, backed by two VCs; no EU subsidiary yet. The plant would cost about EUR 25M, with 60 jobs in year one; we have not chosen the site and have not signed anything. We also want UK support for the export side.

## Wincent's answer

# Public money for your EU plant: Germany, northern Italy, the EU and the UK

I had no access to our internal funding database for this run. Everything below comes from official and secondary web sources I read today. Where I could not confirm a point it is marked (unverified), with a way to check it.

## Answer

**No, the location should not decide this on its own.** The largest public money for a heat-pump component plant does not depend on the site:
- In Germany, a federal clean-tech manufacturing framework allows grants of up to 40% of eligible costs, capped at €200M per company [w3]. It applies anywhere in Germany [w4].
- At EU level, the Innovation Fund has a clean-tech manufacturing topic that names heat pumps and their components [w6].

Regional aid only adds a layer in assisted areas. So choose two or three sites on customers, skills, energy and logistics first. Then let the aid package rank that shortlist.

**Today Germany is the more open door.** Italy's Net Zero window covered heat pumps [w1], but it stopped taking applications on 21 April 2026 [w1]. Most of northern Italy gets little regional aid (judgement).

**On the UK side,** UK Export Finance supports UK exports, not an EU factory. Its fit depends on Sheffield still shipping parts into the EU (judgement).

## Where you stand

**Germany**
- The federal framework (Bundesregelung Transformationstechnologien) covers final net-zero products "and the most important specific components" of them [w3]. It runs until 31.12.2030 [w3].
- But you apply under a specific programme guideline, not the framework itself [w3]. Which open guideline covers heat-pump components outside the coal regions is still to confirm.
- One federal programme (STARK FK12) funds new net-zero manufacturing capacity, but only in the coal regions (unverified, [w5]). That is the one place where the site itself opens a door.
- The federal-state regional aid scheme (GRW) adds money only in assisted areas. The rates by company size are unverified.

**Northern Italy**
- Invitalia's development contracts are open to "Imprese italiane ed estere di ogni dimensione" (Italian and foreign companies of any size) [w2]. The minimum investment is €20M [w2], and your €25M plant clears it.
- The Net Zero window is closed [w1]. Watch for it reopening.

**EU**
- The 2025 Innovation Fund call offered €1bn for manufacturing projects with capex above €2.5M [w6]. It closed on 23 April 2026 with 358 applications [w6].
- The 2026 call is expected to open in December 2026 with an April 2027 deadline (unverified, secondary source [w7]).

**UK**
- The General Export Facility (GEF) is a partial UKEF guarantee to your bank on trade finance. It fits facilities up to around £25M, for companies whose UK export sales were at least 20% of turnover in any one of the last three years [w9]. You apply through your bank [w9].
- The Export Development Guarantee starts at £25M [w8], which is too big for you today (judgement).

## Flags

1. **Undertaking in difficulty (high).** A loss-making company whose accumulated losses exceed half its share capital, share premium included, is excluded from most EU state aid (unverified; confirm in Article 2(18) of the EU block exemption regulation (GBER) on EUR-Lex).
   - The test probably covers the group, including the UK parent (judgement).
   - Fix: check this against your last accounts now. If you are close, close an equity round before you file.
2. **Start of works (high).** You must apply before work begins [w3]. Do not sign machine orders, a building contract or a binding lease until each scheme has your application (judgement on what counts as starting work).
3. **SME status (medium).** On your own figures you are an SME: 80 staff and €14M revenue, plus 60 new jobs, stays under 250 people (judgement). Large VC stakes can count as partner enterprises and change that (unverified). Your lawyers should run the SME test on the cap table.
4. **Clawback (medium).** The investment must be kept for 5 years, or 3 for SMEs [w3]. Build that into the site choice.
5. **Regulatory (flag for Regina).** The F-gas, ecodesign and energy-labelling rules and UK-EU rules of origin affect what you build where. I am handing these to Regina and not analysing them here.

## The money for each cost

For a €25M plant (illustrative, judgement):

| Money | Cost it carries | Why cheap or dear | Illustrative size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grant (German framework, Innovation Fund) | Buildings and machinery | Cheapest: no dilution. Slow, competitive, clawback risk. | 20–40% of eligible costs, so €5–10M ([w3] for the cap) |
| Loan with public backing | The rest of the capex | Mid-cost: the public side takes part of the risk | €8–10M |
| VC equity | At least 25% own contribution [w3], plus ramp-up losses | Dearest: dilution and VC target returns | €6–8M |
| UKEF GEF | Sheffield's export working capital | Cheap: the state takes part of the bank's risk [w9] | Your real number needed |

On combining sources: the German framework allows combination with other aid within state-aid ceilings [w3]. Whether the Innovation Fund can sit on the same cost is unverified.

## Decisions

1. **Site approach.** Run a real comparison of two or three sites across Germany, possibly including a coal region, and one in Italy. I recommend this: it also documents that the aid affects where you invest (judgement).
2. **When to create the EU entity.** Incorporate the subsidiary early, since it costs little, but start no works (judgement).
3. **What Sheffield makes.** Decide whether Sheffield keeps the high-value parts and ships them to the EU plant. That keeps UKEF in play (judgement).

## Precedents

- **Innovation Fund:** the Commission's project list shows an Austrian industrial heat-pump project (GRIP) under the 2024 manufacturing call (unverified, from a search snippet).
- **German framework, Italian Net Zero window, UKEF GEF:** I found no heat-pump plant precedent for any of them. That makes these first-of-a-kind asks, and the case to each funder must be stronger.

## Watchlist

- Innovation Fund 2026 call opening (December 2026 [w7]).
- A German guideline under the framework opening for components.
- The Net Zero window reopening in Italy (Italy's €1.5bn clean-tech scheme was approved under the same EU framework, unverified).
- The regional aid maps in both countries end on 31.12.2027 (unverified for Germany).

2. UK export share in each of the last three years? This decides the GEF.
3. Raising in 2027? A round could double as your own contribution to the plant.
