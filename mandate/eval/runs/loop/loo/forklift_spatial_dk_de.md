# Cheaper capital for the deploy-first model, and public equity in the Series A

## The answer

**Denmark is the open door, not Bavaria.** The Danish ApS signs the contracts, builds the hardware and carries it on its balance sheet until go-live. So the company that needs financing is Danish, and in practice an exporter. Denmark's state fund EIFO can back the ApS's own bank for exactly this: up to 80% of the loss on a working-capital or order-specific credit line [w1]. That is the state taking the risk off your bank, not off your customer. Your customers' terms stay as they are (judgement).

For the round, EIFO can also invest directly as a minority co-investor next to your lead [w3]. Raise that with your lead this month: four months is tight for a second due diligence (judgement).

## Why this is cheaper (the money logic)

- **Today you pay for deployments with equity, which is the dearest money you have.** Investors price it against a venture return, and every euro tied up in hardware is dilution (judgement).
- **A bank lends this cheaply only if it can price the risk.** It cannot price your projects: assets sit at customer sites and payment depends on go-live. That mispricing is the market failure EIFO exists to fix (judgement).
- **What the guarantee costs:** EIFO's premium typically runs 0.8% to 6.3% a year on the guaranteed amount, depending on your rating, plus a fixed fee of DKK 10,000 to 20,000 [w1]. That should still be well below the cost of equity (judgement).
- **Worked example on assumed numbers:** say a deployment ties up EUR 250k for five months. Then a EUR 3M guaranteed revolving line turns over about 2.4 times a year and funds around EUR 7M of deployments, with no Series A money spent on inventory (judgement). Send me your real figures (cost per deployment, months from spend to payment) and I'll redo it.

## Flags

1. **Positive equity is a hard condition for the EIFO-Guarantee [w1].** Close the round first, or put the facility up for approval on the strength of the closing (judgement).
2. **Bavarian money needs a Bavarian borrower.** Do not restructure for it now. Revisit only if German volume grows enough to book German contracts in the GmbH. That needs tax and transfer-pricing advice first (judgement).
3. **Regulatory side, for Regina.** Spatial-awareness systems on forklifts may count as safety components under the new EU Machinery Regulation. If they rely on AI-based person detection, the AI Act may also apply. This affects how funders read the risk. I'll pass it to Regina rather than assess it myself (unverified).

## Decisions for you

1. **Ask EIFO into the round: yes**, but only with your lead's agreement and without delaying the close. If EIFO can't decide in time, keep it for an extension (judgement).
2. **Ask your lead whether its own fund has EIF, EIFO or KfW Capital money behind it.** If so, public capital is already in the round and that relationship can help with EIFO (judgement).
3. **Size of the guaranteed line.** I'd anchor it on 12 months of deployment pipeline.
